Does defense win championships?

    • Offense (rank in points scored)
    • Defense (rank in points allowed)

    Why I started this project

    Growing up, coaches would always say, “Defense wins championships,” and at the time I blindly believed it. Recently, I got into a debate with my roommate: would you rather have your favorite NFL team have the best offense in the league or the best defense? I chose to believe what my coaches instilled in me growing up, while he argued he’d rather have the best offense. To settle the debate, I looked at all 60 Super Bowl champions to find out which has truly mattered more for winning a Super Bowl.

    Conclusion

    After looking at all 60 Super Bowls, the data backs up what my coaches told me, just not as simply as I expected. Teams that reached the Super Bowl with the league’s #1 defense won 17 of 23 times (74%), while teams with the #1 offense won only 11 of 25 (44%). That pattern held at every level I checked. Whether I looked at the top 3, top 5, or top 10, elite defenses won the Super Bowl more often than elite offenses. The clearest evidence came from the 33 Super Bowls where one team had the better defense and the other had the better offense. The team with the better defense won 21 of them.

    That said, defense alone does not win championships. 43 of the 60 champions ranked in the top 10 in both offense and defense, so most winners were elite on both sides of the ball. With only 60 games to look at and a long future of the NFL ahead of us, the results also are not strong enough to call it a rule. What the data does show is that when two good teams meet, the one with the better defense usually comes out on top. So for now, I am calling this debate in my favor.

    How the data was built

    Ranks were calculated from the final score of every regular-season game since 1966, then each Super Bowl team was compared with every team in its league that season.

    • Offense rank is by total points scored (1 = most). Defense rank is by total points allowed (1 = fewest). Playoff games are not counted.
    • Before the 1970 merger the AFL and NFL played separate schedules, so 1966 to 1969 teams are ranked within their own league.
    • Tied teams share the better rank.
    • Sources: FiveThirtyEight game results for 1966 to 1998 and nflverse for 1999 to 2025. The two sources agree on every team for the 22 seasons they overlap, and all 60 Super Bowl scores match the official results.
